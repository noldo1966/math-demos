# Kerr Landau-Siegel proof evaluation, Step 1: structure map and foundation inventory

**Document evaluated:** Bryce Kerr, "A simpler proof that there are no Landau–Siegel zeros," arXiv:2610.21099v1 [math.NT], 15 October 2026, 6 pages (the copy Arnoldo attached on 18 October 2026).

**Standard applied:** a proof must carry the reader from step to step with the reader supplying nothing, judged only by what is on the page. Every result taken from outside the paper must be stated up front as foundation.

**Scope of this step:** a map of what the paper proves, what each part depends on, and every outside result it uses, whether it names that result or not. This step judges no step to be correct or incorrect. Breaks and false steps are catalogued in Step 2. Citations are checked against the originals in Step 4.

## 1. Parameters and the order in which they must be chosen

The proof uses the following parameters. The paper never lists them in one place. The order below is what the proof of Theorem 1 needs, read off its final paragraph ("provided γ sufficiently large, ε sufficiently small in terms of γ, and |q| sufficiently large").

| Order | Parameter | Definition on the page | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | C₁ | implied constant in Lemma 1 (absolute) | nothing |
| 1 | γ | positive integer, "sufficiently large" | C₁ (implicitly) |
| 2 | ε | "sufficiently small in terms of γ" | γ, C₁ |

| 4 | N | \|q\|^γ | γ, q |
| 5 | B | 8N√\|q\| | N, q |

The final constant c of Theorem 1 is never named on the page. It must come from ε (for large |q|) together with a separate treatment of small |q|, which the page does not contain. Step 2 records this.

### 2.2 Logical chain of the main proof

1. Assume (1 − β) log\|q\| < ε.
2. Choose γ, then N, B, D, Q, S.
3. Mertens (F5) and Lemma 1 give Σ_{p∈S} (log p)/p ≥ log Q − O(log\|q\| + (1 − β)(log Q)²).
4. Use log Q = (4/3) log N + O(log log N) and log B = log N + ½ log\|q\| + O(1) to get (9). The working margin is 4/3 − 13/10 = 1/30 in the coefficient of log N.
5. Lemma 5 gives a nonzero F ∈ L_D vanishing at all N⁴ points.
6. The points are n₁γ₁ + … + n₄γ₄ with 0 ≤ n_j ≤ N − 1. Lemma 6 (n = 3, M = N − 1) and Lemma 7 give deg F ≥ ((N − 1)/3)^{4/3}.
7. F ∈ L_D forces deg F ≤ D < ((N − 1)/3)^{4/3}. Contradiction.

### 2.3 The internal margin chain

Three constants must sit in the order 5/4 < 13/10 < 4/3:

- **5/4** comes from the pigeonhole in Lemma 5: W ~ 5D⁴/6144 and p·E_p ~ D⁴/1536, and the ratio of the leading constants is (5/6144)/(1/1536) = 5/4. This is what (11) requires in the limit.
- **13/10** is the threshold the paper chooses in (9). It leaves room of 1/20 for the error terms in Lemma 5.
- **4/3** is log Q / log N in the limit. It comes from D ≍ N^{4/3}, which in turn is set by the exponent µ ≥ 4/3 that Lemmas 6 and 7 supply.

The multiplicative constant (M/n) in Lemma 6 is not load-bearing. A different constant changes D by a constant factor, which changes log Q only by O(1). The exponent µ(Γ) **is** load-bearing. An exponent below 13/10 would break the argument.

## 4. Observations from this step (structure only; no verdicts)

1. **Two results carry the proof from outside:** Lemma 1 (F1) and Lemma 6 (F4). Everything else is either proved on the page or is standard algebra and elementary prime-number estimates.
2. **Lemma 6 is the decisive import.** Its exponent fixes the 4/3 at the top of the margin chain (Section 2.3). The paper gives no statement of where in Masser's paper the result appears (no theorem number), so even locating it is left to the reader.
3. **The paper does not state its foundation up front.** The "Analytic input" paragraph introduces Lemma 1, and Lemma 6 is introduced at the point of use. Under our standard this is the first break; Step 2 will record it with the others.
4. **The conclusion is incomplete as a structure.** The proof by contradiction covers only |q| large, depending on γ and ε. No sentence covers the remaining finitely many q or names c.
