**How to read the answers.**

- **Accepted** means the finding was right when it was raised. Where the text was later corrected, the row says "Fixed in revision N." Otherwise the answer supplies what is missing, so the reader does not have to supply it.
- **Answered** means the finding does not hold, and the answer says why from the page or the sources.
- "Accepted in part," "Accepted as a reading" and "Accepted as wording" mean Accepted, with the limit stated. A row in Part 2 has no label because none of its findings shows a defect in the paper. A row in Parts 3.1, 3.2 or 3.5 with no label supplies what the reader needed; where it says "Fixed in revision N," it counts as Accepted.
- "Record 3.4" means Part 3.4 of this record, and "Section 3.4" means the paper's section. Where a row gives a bare number, the column says which: in Part 4.1, "Where answered" gives parts of this record. "Record A1" means a Part A finding in Part 1; "A1" alone means the paper's foundation item. "C.1" (with a period) is a section of Appendix C; "C1" is a reviewer. In Part 1's first column, A1 to A7 number the Part A findings; inside the words of Part 1's finding A5, "A1 to A7" are the 07:48 reply's labels. In a Finding column, a bare number such as "5.6" is a section of the paper; "A.12" is a section of Appendix A, and "A12" a foundation item.

**Section 4.3, "the same rule."** Several reviewers ask how a standard that gives no one latitude can weigh more on AI because AI lacks latitude (C2, C3, C4). The answer is that "the same rule" in Section 4.3 and in Section 10 means the field's convention, not the standard. Under the convention as I read it (Section 4.3), readers let a presenter with a record of answering skip steps. AI output has no such record, so under the convention it gets no latitude. Under the standard no one gets latitude. The standard therefore asks the same of everyone, but it changes practice most for AI output, because AI output is the case the convention cannot excuse. Fixed in revision 65: Section 4.3 now says "the same convention."

**Section 4.3, "of this kind" and volume.** The declaration dates the abilities at issue to "the last few months." "Of this kind" means proofs produced with those abilities; no presenter can have answered for them over years (C4, G1). "Output can arrive faster than readers can fill in skipped steps" claims a possibility, supported by ten results in one report. It does not claim a measured rate (C3, G1). That such output can outpace readers is my reading.

**Section 7.2 and the Group's three clauses** (G1). The Group's norm names understanding, verification and responsibility. My test addresses all three only if understanding is shown by answering. Since revision 64, requirement 3 makes that explicit: the presenter answers from their own understanding and can check each answer. In my reading, checking each answer is the verification the clause asks for. Whether the test places a result in the Group's course 2.A, rather than in a category the Group does not name, is for the Group to decide (Section 9.2 proposes it).

**Section 7.5, answering is not directing** (C1, C2, C3). The Group's concern is that labs not direct the community's work of understanding. A presenter who answers does not choose the questions; the community does. Section 7.5 adds that the community judges whether the answers hold. A presenter could still shape understanding through its answers; that the record (Section 9.1, item 4) lets others see and dispute them is my answer here, not in the paper.

**Section 7.6, no access to the model** (C1, C3). For questions about how a result was produced, the reader depends on the presenter's answer, which is Section 7.6's point; Section 9.1, item 5 asks for the disclosures that let others check it.

**Section 6.1, volume** (G1, G3). The standard sets no limit on the number of releases (Section 6.1). It bears on volume only through requirement 3: a presenter who releases more than they can answer for, from their own understanding, fails it. Section 6.1 calls this a requirement on presenters, not a limit that enforces itself.

### 3.2 The definitions and the duty

**Consequence 1, "can be resolved by answering"** (C1, C2, C4). Pointing to where a step is on the page supplies the step asked for. The consequence says disagreement "can be" resolved, not that it will be. Once the step is on the page, each reader can check it, and a reader who still disagrees has a new question to put.

**"Answered for" and time to answer** (C4). Accepted. Read literally, "has answered every question put so far" fails the moment a question is put. The intended reading: every question put has been answered or is being answered. What shows the condition is not met is a question left unanswered. Fixed in revision 65.

**Who holds the duty in course 2.B** (C2, C4). The lab that releases a course 2.B result is its presenter and holds the duty. Since revision 64, the duty is to answer from one's own understanding, which no one has yet for a course 2.B result ("not yet understood by anybody"). The result is therefore not answered for until someone who can meet the duty takes it on.

**Section 7.3, "a person questions, the AI answers"** (C4). Since revision 64 this needs a gloss. In Section 5 the AI drafted the answers. The method is that I, as presenter, accept an answer only once I can check it; Appendix C.4 says the rule was not always kept. I own every claim in the paper (Appendix C.1). The presenter's answer is the one the presenter accepts and can explain. Fixed in revision 65: Section 7.3 now says the AI drafts answers, and the person accepts an answer only when the person can check it.

**Section 7.3, "nothing is accepted until the missing step is on the page," against Appendix C.4** (C4). Section 7.3 describes the method shown in Section 5. Appendix C.4 says the rule was not always kept across the whole collaboration, and C.3 lists where.

**Section 8.3 cites Section 9.4 for "owns every answer"** (C1, C2, C4). Since revision 64, Section 9.4 says the person "understands every answer given in their name." "Owns every answer" follows from requirement 3: the presenter answers, from their own understanding, whoever drafted the words. Fixed in revision 65: Section 8.3 now cites Section 4.1, requirement 3, and Section 9.4.

**Question and tone as stipulations** (G1). Accepted. They are stipulated. Section 3 defines question, Section 4.1 states the rule on tone, and Section 4.2 gives where the duty comes from; none argues that the definition or the rule is right. They are my position.
